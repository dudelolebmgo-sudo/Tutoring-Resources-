The Maya

Study guide · Social Studies · Ancient Mesoamerica

Big question

Why would a culture believe that humans were made of corn?

The Popol Vuh is the sacred book of the K'iche' Maya. Its name means roughly “Book of the Community” or “Book of the People” (vuh means “book”). In its creation story, the gods first try to make people out of mud, then out of wood, and both fail. Finally they shape people out of maize (corn) dough, and it works.

Why corn? Because maize was the center of Maya life: it was their main food, farming followed its seasons, and their religion had a maize god. A people whose whole world depended on corn told a story in which they were made of it.

📍 Where did they live?

The Maya lived in southern Mexico (the Yucatán Peninsula and Chiapas) and northern Central America: Guatemala, Belize, and parts of Honduras and El Salvador. Their land had three main regions:

🌽 Food, farming and trade

Main crops: maize, beans and squash, plus chili peppers and cacao (the bean chocolate comes from).

Slash-and-burn farming: farmers cut down a patch of forest, burned it, and planted in the ashes, which made the soil richer. After a few years the soil wore out, so they moved to a new patch. They also built terraces on hillsides and raised fields in swampy land.

Trade: Kings and merchants traded goods like salt, jade, obsidian (volcanic glass for tools), feathers and cotton cloth. Cacao beans were so valuable they were sometimes used like money.

👑 Social organization

  1. The king: called the “holy lord.” People believed he was connected to the gods. He led in war, religion and trade.
  2. Nobles and priests: royal family, officials, scribes and priests who led ceremonies and kept the calendars.
  3. Merchants and artisans: traders, potters, sculptors, weavers and builders.
  4. Farmers and workers: most Maya people. They grew food, paid tribute and helped build temples.
  5. Enslaved people: often prisoners captured in war, or people being punished for crimes.

🛕 Religion and calendars

The Maya worshipped many gods connected to nature, like Chaak (god of rain), Itzamnaaj (a creator god) and the Maize God. They built tall pyramid-temples, held ceremonies and offerings, and played a sacred ballgame.

Tzolk'in: 260 days

13 numbers × 20 day names. This was the sacred calendar used to plan ceremonies. A person's birthday on the Tzolk'in was believed to shape their life and was often part of their name.

Haab': 365 days

18 months of 20 days, plus 5 extra days thought to be unlucky. This solar calendar followed the seasons and the farming year.

The two calendars ran together like gears. The same combination of dates came back only once every 52 years.

✍️ Writing, numbers and science

The Maya had the most complete writing system in the ancient Americas. Their hieroglyphs mixed signs for whole words with signs for syllables. They carved them on stone monuments and painted them in folding books called codices.

Diego de Landa was a Spanish Franciscan friar in Yucatán (he later became its bishop). In 1562, in the town of Maní, he burned many Maya books because he saw them as against his religion; only four codices survive today. Later he wrote a book about the Maya that included a “Maya alphabet.” His alphabet was mistaken (Maya writing isn't an alphabet), but in 1952 the Russian scholar Yuri Knorozov used it as a key to show that many glyphs stand for sounds. That helped people start reading Maya writing again.

The Maya counted in 20s and were one of the first peoples to use zero. They tracked the Sun, Moon and Venus very accurately.

0 = shell
1 = dot
5 = bar
7 = 2 dots + bar

📉 Decline, and the Maya today

  • Between about AD 800 and 900, people left most of the big southern cities. Historians think several causes worked together: long droughts, too many people for the land, cutting down forests, and wars between cities.
  • The Maya did not disappear. Northern cities like Chichén Itzá kept going.
  • The Spanish conquest began in the 1500s. The last independent Maya kingdom fell in 1697.
  • Today, millions of Maya people live in Mexico, Guatemala, Belize, Honduras and El Salvador, and speak about 30 Mayan languages.

⚠️ Watch out for these mix-ups

  • Popol Vuh is the name of a book. Vuh means “book,” not “corn.” The corn is in the story.
  • Tikal, Palenque and Copán are rainforest cities of the southern lowlands, not the highlands.
  • Tzolk'in (260) = sacred calendar for ceremonies and birthdays. Haab' (365) = solar calendar for seasons and farming.
  • Spelling: highlands, lowlands, slash-and-burn farming, priests, nobles.
